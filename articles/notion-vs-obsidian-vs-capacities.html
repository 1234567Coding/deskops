Notion vs. Obsidian vs. Capacities: Best Note-Taking App for Remote Workers

By DeskOps Team · Published September 15, 2026 · 10 min read

Feature checklists won't tell you which note-taking app fits your work. So we skipped the checklist and ran the same three remote-work workflows through Notion, Obsidian, and Capacities for two weeks each: (1) meeting notes with action items that get followed up, (2) a personal knowledge base of research and ideas, and (3) a shared team wiki. Each app won one workflow outright — and lost the other two for interesting reasons.

The 3 Workflows We Tested

We set up each app the way a remote worker actually would: a daily meeting-notes template with an action-item tracker, a research library of ~200 imported notes with cross-links, and a mock team wiki with onboarding docs, runbooks, and a project hub. Then we did real work in each for ten working days — capturing notes live on calls, searching for old decisions under time pressure, and sharing pages with a "teammate" to test collaboration friction.

Notion: Best for Team Wikis and Structured Projects

Notion won the team wiki workflow by a mile. Databases are the reason: a project hub where each project is a row with status, owner, due date, and linked meeting notes is something neither competitor can replicate without plugins. Sharing is frictionless — send a link, set permissions, done — and the new AI features summarize long threads into briefs competently. On calls, the meeting-notes template with a linked action-item database meant follow-ups stopped falling through the cracks.

The trade: Notion is slow. Cold starts take 3–5 seconds, offline mode is unreliable (a real problem on flaky apartment wifi), and the personal knowledge base felt like filing paperwork — every note wants a database home. If your work is collaborative and structured, these are acceptable costs. If it's personal and exploratory, they're dealbreakers.

Obsidian: Best for Personal Knowledge and Offline Work

Obsidian won the personal knowledge base workflow and it wasn't close. Local-first markdown files mean instant search across thousands of notes, zero loading spinners, and your data sitting in a folder you actually own. The graph view sounds gimmicky until you use backlinks for three weeks and realize you've built a thinking tool, not a filing cabinet. The plugin ecosystem (Dataview, Templater, Excalidraw) extends it into a legitimate power tool.

The trade: sharing is painful. Publishing requires the $8/mo Publish add-on or exporting, real-time collaboration doesn't exist, and the learning curve is real — expect a weekend of setup before it feels natural. Our "teammate" test was the weakest of the three: sending an Obsidian vault to a colleague is like handing them your filing cabinet and saying "good luck."

Capacities: Best for Networked Thinkers Who Hate Folders

Capacities is the dark horse. It's object-based: people, projects, books, and meetings are typed objects with properties, linked naturally instead of filed in folders. Our meeting-notes workflow shone here — link a note to the "Project" object and it automatically appears on the project's page, no database configuration required. It feels like the tool was designed by someone who actually takes notes for a living.

The trade: it's the smallest company of the three, which is a real longevity consideration for your life's notes (mitigated by markdown export). The mobile app lags behind Notion's, and team features are newer and thinner. It's the best pure thinking tool of the three and the weakest collaboration tool.

Which Should You Choose?

Run this decision in order:

  1. Do teammates need to read and edit your notes? Yes → Notion. Collaboration is its moat; the others can't touch it.
  2. Is this primarily your private thinking space? Yes → Obsidian if you value speed, ownership, and offline access; Capacities if you hate folders and want linking to feel effortless.
  3. Do you live in meetings? Pair your pick: Notion for the team wiki + action tracking, and keep a personal scratchpad in Obsidian or Capacities. Plenty of remote workers run two tools — one shared, one private — and that's fine.

The wrong answer is spending a month migrating 2,000 notes between apps instead of doing your work. Pick based on the workflow you do most, commit for 30 days, and only then optimize.

FAQ

Can I migrate notes between them?
Mostly yes. All three import/export markdown, so plain notes move cleanly. What doesn't transfer: Notion databases (become flat pages elsewhere), Obsidian plugin setups (Dataview queries don't run in other apps), and Capacities object types (flatten to tags). Budget a weekend for a big migration.
Which has the best mobile app?
Notion, by a clear margin — full-featured and reliable. Obsidian mobile is solid for capture and review but plugin-dependent. Capacities mobile is the weakest; fine for quick capture, frustrating for deep work.
Is my data safe in these apps?
Obsidian's local files are yours, full stop — back them up like any important folder. Notion and Capacities are cloud services: export your workspace quarterly (both support it) so you're never locked in.
Can I use one for work and keep personal notes separate?
Yes, and you should. Use separate workspaces/vaults for work and personal. It keeps employer data boundaries clean and prevents your employer's IT policy from touching your personal notes.
